Screens from a real tablet running Muralis 0.7. Nothing here is drawn.

It tells you how it is doing

Switch on the system stats and the panel draws them over its own page: processor, memory, temperature, battery, network, uptime. Move the block where it covers nothing. Over an app it works the same.

Everything a wall screen needs

All of it is free, on every panel you own, except what only makes sense across panels.

On the wall

  • One page, full screen. No browser bar, no Android bars, no way to another app.
  • App lock. One app you choose instead of a page, opened again when it closes.
  • The way out. Corner taps you record yourself, and a PIN after them if you want.
  • Pop-ups. A message, a picture, a camera or a page over the screen, for some seconds.
  • Status light. A coloured frame around the screen, switched like a light.

Keeps working

  • Retries a page that failed, behind a screen that names the address it cannot reach.
  • Reloads a page that froze. One that never changes is left alone.
  • Rebuilds a crashed browser engine, and again when memory runs low.
  • Restarts itself every night, and is back by itself after a reboot.

Display and Ambient

  • Display off, done properly. A real sleep where the tablet can be trusted, a black film where not.
  • Ambient. A dimmed page, black, another page, or your pictures in playlists.
  • Brightness and orientation, from the panel, the browser or Home Assistant.
  • Page options. A sign-in, text size, zoom, a dark look, camera and microphone.
  • System stats over the page: memory, temperature, battery, network, uptime.

Senses the room

  • Sensors. Light, proximity, movement, battery, power, network and more.
  • Camera. The tablet's own camera as a network camera: motion detection, a stream and a snapshot.
  • Sound level from the microphone. Nothing is recorded.
  • Bluetooth beacons and NFC tags. Which beacons are near, and which tag touched it.
  • Automations. When this, then that, on the panel itself, at a time of day too.

From your browser

  • Web admin. Every setting from a browser, served by the panel over HTTPS.
  • HTTP API. The same commands, each answered with the panel's real reply.
  • MQTT with Home Assistant discovery, over TLS if your broker offers it.
  • A picture of the screen and a diagnostics file, when you ask for them.
  • Settings file and setup QR. Copy one panel's settings to the next.

TogetherPro

  • The fleet. Up to six panels, managed from any of them.
  • Add a panel by its camera reading a code, or by typing the code.
  • Share what should be the same, with every panel or with a group.
  • Alerts. A panel offline, a page error, a low battery: said once.
  • History and every screen. Restore an older version; see each panel's screen.

Keep the battery in a healthy band

A tablet plugged in forever sits at 100 percent, warms up, and after a year or two the battery swells. This charges it only between two thresholds; only the charger is switched.

  • the panel’s battery level sensor
  • the smart plug feeding its charger
  • the floor and the ceiling, 20 and 80 by default

Two ways onto a tablet

They are not the same. Only the QR makes a locked panel; the app from Google Play is for trying it.

By QR, the full panel

Muralis owns the tablet and holds the screen on your page. Android grants that only during setup, which is why the tablet has to be reset first.

  1. Back up anything on the tablet.
  2. Factory reset, and stop at the welcome screen.
  3. Tap the screen six times, then join a network when it asks.
  4. Scan the code. A tablet that cannot verify the code’s signing fingerprint refuses to go on.
Open the download page

From Google Play, to try it

The app only. No lock-down, nothing is wiped: Android grants ownership during setup and never after. Good for seeing what it does before you reset a tablet.

Nothing hidden: the APK, its checksum and its signing certificate are published on the download page.
