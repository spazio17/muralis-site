Android kiosk for any web page or app

Muralis

Hang it up, and leave it.

Muralis turns an Android tablet into a wall panel for any web page or app: a dashboard, a board, a menu, a page on your own network. It holds the screen, recovers by itself, and listens to your network over the web admin, HTTP and MQTT. No cloud, no account, no server.

Who it is for

One screen, one page, nobody looking after it

Muralis does not care what is on the page. If a browser can show it, a tablet on the wall can hold it.

Smart-home dashboards

Home Assistant, openHAB, ioBroker, Domoticz. To Muralis they are all a page.

Office and workshop boards

A Grafana board, a build monitor, a ticket queue, on the wall of the room that needs it.

Front desk and shop

A welcome screen, a menu, today’s schedule, on a tablet nobody has to babysit.

Your own network

An intranet page or a local server. Nothing has to be on the internet.

What it does

Built to be left alone

Wall panels drift. A browser stops repainting, a network blip leaves an error page, an update reboots into the stock launcher. Muralis handles each of those without anyone walking over.

Locked to one page

Home app, lock task, no system UI to reach. Back by itself after a reboot.

Recovers unattended

Failed loads retry behind a screen that names the address it cannot reach, hung loads time out, a dead renderer is rebuilt.

Catches a frozen page

A page that was updating and stopped is reloaded. One that never changes is left alone.

Restarts itself nightly

A full restart in the small hours, and a rebuild whenever memory runs low.

A way out you choose

Three to twelve corner taps, recorded on the device. Nothing is hardcoded.

Display off, done properly

A real screen-off where the tablet can be trusted to sleep, a black film where it cannot. It says which.

Something to look at when nobody is

Dimmed page, black film, another page, or pictures: your folders in playlists.

An app, if not a page

App lock shows one app you choose instead of a page, and opens it again when it closes. Your corner taps still work over it.

At a glance

The panel tells you how it is doing

Switch on the system stats and the panel draws them over its own page: memory, temperature, battery, network, uptime. Move the block where it does not cover anything. Over an app it works the same.

Everything it does

All of it, on one page

What a Muralis panel does today, in four groups. Everything here is free on one panel, except where it says Pro.

On the wall

  • One page, full screen. No browser bar, no Android bars, no way to another app.
  • App lock. One app instead of a page, opened again when it closes.
  • The way out. Corner taps you record, a PIN after them if you want.
  • Self-recovery. Retries, frozen pages, a crashed engine, a nightly restart.
  • Display. Brightness, orientation, a real screen-off or a black film.
  • Ambient. A dimmed page, black, another page, or your pictures in playlists.
  • Pop-ups. A message, a picture, a camera or a page over the screen, for some seconds.
  • Status light. A coloured frame around the screen, switched like a light.
  • Page options. A sign-in for the page, text size, zoom, a dark look, camera and microphone.

Sense and act

  • Sensors. Light, proximity, movement, battery, power, network and more.
  • Camera. A network camera with motion detection, a stream and a snapshot.
  • Sound level. From the microphone. Nothing is recorded.
  • Bluetooth beacons and NFC tags. Who is near, and which tag touched it.
  • Automations. When this, then that, on the panel itself, at a time of day too.
  • Actions. Wake, dim, a pop-up, a message to MQTT, a call to an address.

Control

  • Web admin. Every setting from a browser, served by the panel over HTTPS.
  • HTTP API. The same commands and a live status, with a reference.
  • MQTT. With Home Assistant discovery, over TLS if your broker offers it.
  • Your own certificate. Upload one, or trust the panel’s by its fingerprint.
  • Screenshot and diagnostics. What the wall shows, and a file to send when asking for help.
  • Settings file and setup QR. Copy one panel’s settings to the next, even at its first start.

Together Pro

  • The fleet. Up to six panels, managed from any of them.
  • Add a panel. By its camera reading a QR, or by typing a code.
  • Groups and sharing. Share a section with every panel or with a group.
  • Alerts. A panel offline, a page error, a low battery: said once.
  • History. The last versions of the fleet, and Restore.
  • Every screen on one page. A picture of each panel, and Identify.

The fleet, with Pro

Every panel on one page, served by one of them

Panels on one network trust each other by their own keys. Open the web admin of any of them and you manage all: no cloud, no server, no account.

  • Add a panel in one step. Its camera reads the other panel’s Join QR, or you type the eight digits under it. A new tablet can join at its first start, before anything locks.
  • Share what should be the same. The page, MQTT, the web admin, the display, Ambient, the alerts: one switch each, for the whole fleet or for a group like “Meeting rooms”.
  • One page address for all. {panel.name} in the address gives each panel its own page.
  • Know when something is wrong. Alerts on the panel, to an MQTT topic and to a web address, once when it starts and once when it ends.
  • See every screen. A picture of each panel on request, never stored, and Identify to find which one is which.
  • Change it back. The fleet keeps its last versions; restore one as new.

Remote control

Every setting, from anywhere on your network

The web admin is a page the panel serves itself. The same commands and settings answer over HTTP and MQTT, and the reference shows each one with the panel’s real reply.

  • One command set. The same names over HTTP and MQTT. A refused command says why.
  • Every setting. The page, brightness, orientation, display off, Ambient and its playlists, the broker, the escape combinations.
  • Live status. Battery, memory, storage, network, temperature and page health, as JSON.
  • Telemetry to automate on. Battery, memory, storage, network, CPU, thermal, screen and page health, once a minute and sooner when something changes.
  • Nothing leaves your network, and nothing is on until you set it up. No default credentials; broker and admin secrets encrypted in the Android keystore. No cloud, no account.
Open the reference

     -d cmnd=kiosk.open_url \
     -d url=https://muralis.spazio17.org

In motion

Four clips off a real panel

Recorded over the cable. They loop, there is no sound, and nothing in them is a mock-up.

The page fails and nobody touches the tablet. About ten seconds later the panel loads the configured page again by itself. The waiting runs at five times speed, and the badge counts the real seconds as they pass.

Five taps in one corner opens the settings. You choose the corners on the device, and the page underneath carries on as if nothing happened.

A POST to the panel’s own web admin and it changes page, with nobody near it. The reply under the command is the panel’s own.

curl -k -u :your-admin-password -X POST https://192.0.2.42:8080/api/command \
     -d cmnd=kiosk.open_url \
     -d url=https://muralis.spazio17.org

One message on the panel’s command topic. It does as it is told and answers on its own result topic.

mosquitto_pub -h test.mosquitto.org \
    -t kiosk/hallway-panel/command \
    -m '{"command":"kiosk.open_url","args":{"url":"https://muralis.spazio17.org"}}'

Screens

The tablet, the web admin, and what they produce

For Home Assistant users

Discovered, not declared

Over MQTT the panel appears in Home Assistant as one device, with no YAML: sensors for battery, memory, storage and temperature, controls for brightness, orientation, display off and the page, buttons for reload, restart and reboot. The two recipes below are ready-made automations built on them. Import one and Home Assistant asks you which entities to use.

Wake on presence, blank when empty

A panel that glows all night is a nightlight nobody asked for. This wakes the screen when someone is in the room and blanks it again once the room has been empty for a while. The page stays loaded and comes straight back.

It asks you for

  • a motion or occupancy sensor
  • the panel’s Display on and Display off buttons
  • how long the room must be empty first
  • optionally, something that keeps it dark regardless
Add to Home Assistant On GitHub

Keep the battery in a healthy band

A tablet left plugged in forever sits at 100 percent and warms up, and after a year or two the battery swells and lifts the screen off the front. This charges it only between two thresholds instead. The panel keeps running throughout; only the charger is switched.

It asks you for

  • the panel’s battery level sensor
  • the smart plug feeding its charger
  • the floor and the ceiling, 20 and 80 by default
Add to Home Assistant On GitHub

Both read the panel’s own entities. Home Assistant imports blueprints from GitHub rather than from any website, which is why the buttons above hand it the repository and not this page.

Install

Two ways onto a tablet

They are not the same. Only the QR makes a locked panel; the app from Play is for trying it.

Muralis owns the tablet and holds the screen on your page. Android grants that only during setup, which is why the tablet has to be reset first.

  1. Back up anything on the tablet.
  2. Factory reset, and stop at the welcome screen.
  3. Tap the screen six times, then join a network when it asks.
  4. Scan the code.

A tablet that cannot verify the code’s signing fingerprint refuses to go on. What the code encodes, and how to check it, is on the download page.

The app only. No lockdown, nothing is wiped: Android grants ownership during setup and never after. Good for seeing what it does before you reset a tablet.

Nothing hidden. The APK, its checksum and its signing certificate are published.

Requirements

What it needs

Android 8.0 (API 26) or newer.
Memory 2 GB or more.
Processor What a 2019 tablet has is enough: tested on a Huawei MediaPad T5 and a Lenovo Tab M10.
Install An ordinary APK. No root, no platform signature.
Full kiosk Needs device owner, over adb or by QR at setup.
Without it Still runs and recovers. System UI stays reachable, reboot and real screen-off unavailable.
Battery settings Two switches Android keeps for you. How to set them.
Google account Only to buy Pro. The other panels of the fleet need none.
The page Any web page, on the internet or on your own network.
Broker Only for MQTT. Over TLS if your broker offers it; without it, trusted networks only.
