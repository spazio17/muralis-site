MURALIS PRO

Everything above is free and stays free. Muralis Pro is a one time purchase that unlocks the remote control surfaces: MQTT, including Home Assistant discovery and telemetry, and the local web administration.

Pro belongs to the Google account that bought it, not to a device. Every panel signed in to that account is unlocked, with no limit on how many. It does not expire and there is nothing to renew. It cannot be transferred, resold, or shared with another account.

WHAT THE APP DOES TO THE DEVICE

Muralis is designed to take over a device. When granted device-owner status it will: become the home application, hold the screen on a single task so other applications cannot be reached, disable the notification shade and status bar, disable the lock screen, keep the display awake while charging, and make the back gesture inert.

This is the intended behaviour of a kiosk, and you should understand it before provisioning a device you rely on for anything else.

GETTING BACK OUT

There is always an escape route, and it does not require a cable: a configurable series of corner taps releases the kiosk and returns the system launcher. The default combinations are shown on the configuration screen. Change them, and remember what you changed them to.

REMOVING DEVICE-OWNER STATUS

Device-owner status cannot be granted to an already-configured device, and removing it may require a factory reset. Provision deliberately.

REMOTE CONTROL

The local web administration is authenticated by the password you set, and by nothing else: there is no username, and there is no limit on how many times a wrong password may be tried. MQTT is authenticated by your broker, using whatever credentials it requires, which may be none: Muralis itself does not authenticate individual commands, so anyone who can publish to your broker can control the panel. Both surfaces are UNENCRYPTED, and there is no option to encrypt either. Your broker username and password travel in clear text, so do not reuse a password you use anywhere else. Run these surfaces only on a network you trust, and reach them over a VPN rather than exposing a port. If you set a weak password, or expose these surfaces to the internet, anyone who reaches them can change what the device displays and restart it.
