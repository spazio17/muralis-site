WHAT THE APP DOES TO THE DEVICE

Muralis is designed to take over a device. When granted device-owner status it will: become the home application, hold the screen on a single task so other applications cannot be reached, disable the notification shade and status bar, disable the lock screen, keep the display awake while charging, make the back gesture inert, block starting the device in safe mode, allow only the system's accessibility services and the system's keyboards plus the one in use, and grant itself the permissions its features use (the camera, the microphone, location for the Bluetooth beacons, the photos and notifications), each feature still staying off until you switch it on.

This is the intended behaviour of a kiosk, and you should understand it before provisioning a device you rely on for anything else.

GETTING BACK OUT

There is always an escape route, and it does not require a cable: a series of corner taps that you record at the first start releases the kiosk and returns the system launcher. The combinations are shown in the settings, under Way out. Change them if you like, and remember what you changed them to.

REMOVING DEVICE-OWNER STATUS

Device-owner status cannot be granted to an already-configured device, and removing it may require a factory reset. Provision deliberately.

REMOTE CONTROL

The local web administration is authenticated by the password you set, and by nothing else: there is no username. An address that keeps sending wrong passwords is locked out for a growing while, and the lockout is reported in the status snapshot. It is served over HTTPS with a certificate the panel makes for itself, or with one you upload; with the panel's own your browser will warn about it once, and the panel shows the fingerprint of the certificate in use so you can check that you are talking to your panel. MQTT is authenticated by your broker, using whatever credentials it requires, which may be none: Muralis itself does not authenticate individual commands, so anyone who can publish to your broker can control the panel. MQTT is UNENCRYPTED unless you switch on Encrypted (TLS) for a broker that offers it. Without it, your broker username and password travel in clear text, so do not reuse a password you use anywhere else. Run these surfaces only on a network you trust, and reach them over a VPN rather than exposing a port. If you set a weak password, or expose these surfaces to the internet, anyone who reaches them can change what the device displays and restart it.
